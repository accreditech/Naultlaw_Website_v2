## TN RPC 7.1 watch-outs

- LinkedIn's **"Headline"** field is the single biggest violation risk. Many attorneys write "Specializing in business law" — banned under TN RPC 7.1. Use "Focuses on" or "Practice in" instead.
- LinkedIn's **"Skills"** section lets users (or LinkedIn) tag skills. Avoid the auto-suggested "Specialization" / "Specialty" tags. Use "Business Law," "Real Estate Law," "Contract Drafting," "Commercial Leasing," etc.
- LinkedIn endorsements & recommendations are testimonial-flavored. They DO appear on the profile and are visible to anyone searching the lawyer. Per RPC 7.1 Comment [3], these aren't strictly off-limits, but the lawyer should avoid soliciting recommendations with prompts like "say I'm the best" — endorsements that imply unjustified expectations of similar results in other matters can still implicate Comment [3].
- Activity / posts on LinkedIn — the same compliance rules apply to anything the lawyer publishes. If Steve writes a LinkedIn post saying "won a $500k case for a client," that's a comparative / past-results claim that needs context + disclaimer.
