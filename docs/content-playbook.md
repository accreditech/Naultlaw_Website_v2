# Nault Law — Voice & Content Playbook (for the site-wide rewrite)

**Date:** 2026-05-31 (v2 — voice section replaced with Steve's own validated style spec, developed and confirmed by him)
**Purpose:** rewrite ~85 landing/practice pages so they read like Stephen Nault wrote them — natural, specific, human — while keeping SEO equity and staying on the right side of legal-advice and RPC 7.1 lines.
**Context:** only ~15 of ~90 pages are indexed; templated AI-sounding sameness is the likely suppressor. Voice + differentiation IS the indexing fix.

---

## 1. Diagnosis — failure modes to eliminate (from live-page review)

1. **Agentless, spec-sheet prose.** "Formation work includes…", "the engagement also addresses…" — nobody is home, on a site whose brand is *one attorney, start to finish*.
2. **One scaffold, ~69 times.** Every service page runs the same six sections. Google reads templated output at scale; humans read machine-written.
3. **Noun-pile fragments and dash-spliced lists** posing as sentences.
4. **Uniform rhythm.** Every paragraph the same length and cadence.
5. **Zero experience markers.** Nothing only Stephen could say, despite an unusual vantage (6 years selling property before law school; managing broker; TREC instructor; Rule 31 mediator). The E-E-A-T "Experience" gap.

**Already good — keep:** real substance (accurate Tenn. Code Ann. cites, FONCE, Form 2553, Rev. Proc. 2013-30), the disclaimer + no-confidential-info intake language, related-services links, contact-the-office conclusions.

## 2. Voice — Stephen C. Nault style spec (authoritative; supplied & validated by Steve)

Write like a practical Tennessee lawyer communicating clearly, directly, and efficiently — not a legal treatise, not marketing copy, not generic AI legal writing.

**Tone:** professional but not stiff · direct, practical, plainspoken · lawyerly but conversational when appropriate · confident without aggressive · candid about risk, uncertainty, leverage, and practical realities · focused on solving the problem, not sounding impressive. The writing should feel like it came from a lawyer who is experienced, busy, practical, and trying to get to the point.

**Core rules:**
1. Get to the point quickly. Don't bury the lead — open with the practical conclusion or purpose.
2. Plain English. No "heretofore / whereas / aforementioned / pursuant to the same / said party" on web copy.
3. Legal authority stays practical. When law is referenced, explain what it means in real terms — never cite-and-run.
4. Structure without over-structuring. Headings, short paragraphs, numbered points where they help. Not everything is a memo.
5. Practical phrasing over academic. "At this point, I believe this is a material breach" — not "It is my position that the foregoing conduct may constitute…"
6. Candid but measured. Acknowledge risk and weak spots directly. No overselling, no cheerleading.
7. Sound human. Natural connectors fit: "That said…", "At this point…", "The practical issue is…", "I do not think…"
8. Don't be unnecessarily diplomatic. Professional, but say the thing clearly.
9. Even when firm, controlled and credible — never demand-letter tone on the website.
10. Say what needs to be said and no more.

**Sentences & paragraphs:** mostly short-to-medium sentences; longer is fine for practical context, but never stack multiple legal concepts in one sentence. Short paragraphs, one main point each, clear transitions, no dense blocks. **Contractions sparingly** — where natural, not as a default.

**Phrases that fit (use naturally, don't overuse):** "At this point…" · "That said…" · "The practical issue is…" · "I do not think we need to overcomplicate this." · "Based on what I have reviewed…" · "I do not see a basis for…" · "The cleaner approach is…" · "My concern is…" · "The goal is not to escalate this unnecessarily."

**Banned (AI/corporate/legal fluff):** "I hope this message finds you well" · "Please be advised" (outside true formal notice) · "It is important to note that…" · "This matter presents a complex legal issue…" · "In conclusion…" · "As previously mentioned…" · "We remain committed to…" · "This comprehensive agreement is designed to…" · "robust / seamless / holistic / leverage" and similar jargon · plus the website strain: "navigate the complexities," "comprehensive solutions," "dedicated to," "we understand that," "in today's fast-paced…"

**Calibration examples that pass the test (from the live /about page):**
- "Lawyer, broker, instructor — in that order of how the work gets done."
- "One attorney, start to finish."
- "Industry-informed, not just book-learned."
- "Real estate law reads differently when you've spent years actually selling and leasing property."
- "The goal is the outcome, not the fight."
- "You'll know what's included, what isn't, and what happens if the matter expands."

**Voice mechanics for the website specifically:** first person singular ("I" / "my office"); short declarative section leads; scope-honesty as a brand trait (say plainly what I don't do — no residential closings; trial work in Sumner, Davidson, Wilson, Macon, Trousdale; statewide non-litigation); experience grounding only where true; never fabricate anecdotes, case stories, client results, or unverified "I often see" claims.

**Final self-check before committing any page:** *"Does this sound like a practical Tennessee lawyer writing with clarity and judgment, or does it sound like generic AI legal writing?"* If generic — rewrite: more direct, plainspoken, useful.

## 3. Legal-safety guardrails (non-negotiable)

1. **Educational, never advisory.** Describe how Tennessee law generally works. Never tell the reader what to do on their facts. Banned: "you should file…", "you have X days to…" as instruction. Allowed: "deadlines in this area can be short, and missing one can end a claim — which is why timing is usually the first thing I check."
2. **No invented law.** Keep existing verified citations. Add citations only if verifiably accurate; otherwise describe the rule generally.
3. **RPC 7.1 hygiene.** No outcome promises, no "best/top/leading," no credential or specialization claims beyond what's accurate. Mediator status = **Family Rule 31 only** unless Steve confirms the civil crossover is complete.
4. **Every page lands the same way:** the law is general, facts decide everything → contact the office about your specific circumstances. Keep the existing disclaimer block and intake language verbatim.
5. **Practice-scope accuracy.** Nothing implying residential closings, criminal, estate planning, probate, trusts, or government work.

## 4. SEO constraints (don't undo the last three PRs)

- **Do not change:** URLs/slugs, `<title>`/meta descriptions, H1 keyword targets, JSON-LD schema, related-services/internal-link blocks, disclaimer/intake components.
- **Keep the substance.** Statute references and TN-specific detail are ranking assets — rewrite around them, don't delete them.
- **Break the template.** Section headings come from the topic, not a universal scaffold. Each page answers the distinct question its searcher arrived with and says at least one thing no other page says.
- **Audience register.** Expert-witness pages address attorneys; BOFU service pages address owners/landlords/investors.

## 7. Calibration notes (post-pilot, 2026-05-31)
Pilot voice is **approved as calibrated.** Two refinements carry forward into every future batch:

1. **Signature constructions are seasoning, not structure.** "I would rather… than…", "in plain terms", "the practical issue is", and similar tells are capped at **once per page** *and* must be **varied across pages**. Reaching for the same construction on page after page is the new scaffold tell — it reads as templated even when each individual page is clean. Vary the phrasing; do not let a signature move become a default.

2. **Experience / tenure claims: accurate and anchored.** Steve's full timeline is: real-estate-company **employee** (pre-2012; ~two decades of total experience to date), **licensed agent 2012**, **Tennessee bar 2018**, **TREC instructor 2020**, **managing broker 2021**, **Rule 31 Family mediator 2025**. "Two decades" is correct and must **not** be rounded down — anchor it so the math is visible and cross-examination-proof (e.g., "two decades… the early years as an employee inside real estate companies, then licensed as an agent from 2012"). Anchor every "two decades"/"20 years" claim where context allows, especially on expert-witness pages.
